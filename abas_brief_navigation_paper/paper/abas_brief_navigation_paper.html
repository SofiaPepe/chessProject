Adaptive Functioning, Everyday Executive Functioning, and Baseline Navigational Skills in Children

Author

Nome Autore

Published

July 1, 2026

Abstract

This paper will examine whether everyday adaptive functioning and autonomy, measured with ABAS-II, and everyday executive functioning, measured with BRIEF-2, are associated with children’s baseline navigational and visuospatial abilities. The analysis is cross-sectional and uses only PRE scores from the final database, treating the sample as if no training and no second assessment had occurred.

Introduction

Children’s ability to navigate space develops within everyday relationships, opportunities for movement, and repeated interactions with meaningful environments. A broad developmental perspective suggests that attachment relationships, autonomy-supportive contexts, and embodied motor experience may all contribute to how children explore, plan routes, remember spatial locations, and solve navigation-like problems.

In this preliminary version, the theoretical background is intentionally brief. The final introduction will expand the links among attachment theory, autonomy, motor experience, adaptive functioning, executive functioning, and navigational development.

Methods

Participants

The analytic sample includes all valid participants from the final database used in the larger project. The present paper does not use training group, POST scores, or PRE-to-POST change. All analyses are based on baseline/PRE measures only.

Measures

Children completed baseline cognitive, visuospatial, and Minefield measures. Teacher questionnaires included ABAS-II adaptive functioning scales and BRIEF-2 everyday executive-function scales and indices. ABAS and BRIEF total scores were derived in the analysis pipeline.

Minefield Variables

The Minefield task includes working-memory, planning, and working-memory plus planning conditions. PRE outcome variables include accuracy, span/trial measures, route-efficiency percentages, time measures, error/obstacle-related measures, and derived composite scores. Time variables are also available as log-transformed PRE scores when defined by the shared project pipeline.

Statistical Analysis

The analysis was run with:

python abas_brief_navigation_paper\analysis\run_analysis.py

Descriptive analyses summarized sample composition, missingness, questionnaire scores, and PRE outcomes. Questionnaire-PRE associations were tested with both Pearson and Spearman correlations. Primary regression models predicted each standardized PRE outcome from each standardized ABAS/BRIEF variable, adjusting for standardized age:

outcome_PRE_z ~ questionnaire_z + age_z

HC3 robust standard errors were used. Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction was applied within each result family. The main text reports FDR-significant results only. Additional sensitivity outputs include Minefield-only focus models and models additionally adjusting for Raven PRE accuracy.

Discussion

The discussion will interpret whether teacher-rated autonomy, adaptive functioning, and everyday executive functioning are associated with baseline navigational and visuospatial skills. It will also distinguish associations that appear specific to Minefield navigation outcomes from broader associations with Raven, Corsi, and Tower of London performance.

Limitations to expand include the cross-sectional design, multiple testing, teacher-report measurement, missingness in supplemental ABAS variables, and the need to separate adaptive autonomy from general cognitive development.
